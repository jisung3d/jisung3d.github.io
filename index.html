I am a Senior Research Scientist at NAVER LABS, working on large-scale 3D reconstruction and neural rendering for real-world environments. My work has contributed to NAVER Map Street View 3D and Flying View 3D, as well as city-scale digital twin reconstruction and novel-view synthesis for media and VFX.

My current research focuses on extending reconstructed real-world scenes beyond their original capture trajectories, with the goal of supporting visual simulation for robotics and autonomous driving. I explore 3D Gaussian Splatting, generative video/3D models, and feed-forward 3D reconstruction to improve poorly observed regions and enable consistent rendering along diverse trajectories.

Before joining NAVER LABS in 2022, I worked at Samsung Electronics and Samsung Research on mobile AR, computational photography, and 3D content creation. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from KAIST in 2018, where my research focused on interactive 3D reconstruction using RGB-D and multi-view imagery.

CURRENT RESEARCH

3D Reconstruction for Simulation Beyond Captured Trajectories

Ongoing research

I am exploring how 3D Gaussian Splatting, generative video/3D models, and feed-forward 3D reconstruction can turn real-world observations into reusable 3D environments for visual simulation. The goal is to support diverse trajectories beyond the original capture paths, including viewpoints and regions that are poorly observed or not captured.

A key focus is extending scene geometry and appearance while maintaining multi-view consistency in large-scale outdoor environments. I aim to support simulation for robotics and autonomous driving, with a longer-term interest in connecting persistent 3D scene representations to world models for embodied AI.

A parallel thread asks how far a reconstructed scene can be edited without touching its geometry: changing appearance, lighting, or season while keeping structure and multi-view consistency intact, so one reconstruction can serve many simulation conditions.
